`OPEN_RESOURCE_LIMIT` boundary and move to Phase C, preserving all exact

budget artifact.

that the remaining route requires a materially different solver campaign and
that the current project rules prohibit starting that campaign without a

## Owner choices

### Choice A — authorize a dedicated-CAS campaign

This keeps the one-sided profiles open and creates a new campaign.  Before any
production solver call, the owner must approve:

1. a toolchain (Sage, Singular, Macaulay2, or another specified CAS);
2. a versioned budget artifact, including the existing 3,600-second/chart,
   43,200-second total, and 8 GiB default limits or an explicit amendment;
3. a small reproducibility benchmark against the corrected residual-unit
   preflight;
4. process supervision, hard timeout, memory accounting, and cleanup;
5. a staged input plan that does not launch the 1,967-residual system at once.

Installing or running such a CAS is **not authorized by this packet**.

### Choice B — execute the recommended freeze

Record both proposed `OPEN_RESOURCE_LIMIT` labels as owner-approved terminal
boundaries, preserve the exact evidence above, and transition to Phase C.
This would end Phase-A computation under the mission rules, but it would not
make the finite lattice claim true.

## Consequences of the recommended freeze

- `FINAL_THEORY_OPEN` remains the global verdict;
- `complete finite ON semantics lattice` remains an unavailable claim;
- no `witness certified` or `commutativity obstructed` claim is created for
  either full profile;
- Phase C can turn the verification discipline into a standalone methods
  artifact: semantic digests, claim boundaries, verdict grammar, frozen
  artifacts, preflight/budget separation, and correction provenance;
- the scoped Paper-I / SR3b-A work may continue only under its existing narrow
  claim boundary and owner-deposit gate; it must not be upgraded into a full
  955/721 classification;
- Phase B expansion (`n=5`, `d=3`, singular, OFF, or finite-to-infinite
  lifting) does not start as a way to evade the Phase-A stop rule.
