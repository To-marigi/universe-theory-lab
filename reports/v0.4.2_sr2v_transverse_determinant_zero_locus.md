## 2. Determinant vanishing is not the witness condition

Work in the transverse chart `A_e=[[a_e,x_e],[0,b_e]]`, `Omega=e_2`.  A direct
generic `2 x 2` computation gives the six commutator coefficients as

```text
[Q_i,Q_j]_(12) = x_j*(a_i - b_i) - x_i*(a_j - b_j),
```

and all three other entries vanish. Write `C(delta)` for these six linear forms
and

```text
K_delta = ker C(delta),
delta = (a_Q1-b_Q1, a_Q2-b_Q2, a_Q3-b_Q3, a_Q4-b_Q4).
```

Then `Q_1,...,Q_4` commute exactly when `x_Q` lies in `K_delta`. The important
zero-vector exception is

```text
delta != 0  =>  K_delta = span(delta),
delta  = 0  =>  K_delta = Q^4.
```

Thus “the line spanned by `delta`” is valid only on `delta != 0`; at
`delta=0`, every upper-right `Q` coordinate commutes.

Writing `M` for a branch matrix and `C` for the six commutator rows, a
reachable-visible noncommutative witness exists at a base point if and only if

```text
rank[M;C] > rank M.
```

A determinant-zero point is a point where the *argument* of the principal-open
theorem stops. It is a witness only if the `Q`-projection of `ker M` escapes
`K_delta`. Everything below is organised by that distinction.

## 3. The coboundary direction

Substituting `x_e = beta_e := a_e - b_e` gives, for every edge,

```text
[[a_e, a_e-b_e],[0, b_e]] = C * diag(a_e,b_e) * C^-1,   C = [[1,-1],[0,1]].
```

Because this is a single global conjugation, every word in the generators,
inverses included, becomes `C*diag(A_W,B_W)*C^-1`, so its upper-right entry
telescopes to

```text
upper_right(W) = A_W - B_W.
```

Three consequences follow, and they are exact and universal.

1. An operator relation forces `A_lhs=A_rhs` and `B_lhs=B_rhs`, so its residual
   on `beta` is `(A_lhs-B_lhs)-(A_rhs-B_rhs)=0`.  **`beta` satisfies all 783
   CPOBC rows, both 25-row Eq. (113) readings and all 10 Eq. (139) rows at every
   base point.**
2. A fixed-vector GC relation only forces `B_lhs=B_rhs`, so its residual on
   `beta` is `A_lhs-A_rhs`.  It vanishes exactly when the *upper* character also
   satisfies that monomial row.
3. A reachable-state MSR row evaluates on `beta` to
   `(identity_coefficient + sum coeff*a_e)*A_state`, so it vanishes exactly when
   the upper character also satisfies that additive equation.

Hence

```text
beta in ker M   <=>   the upper character a is itself a bottom scalar locus point.
```

And `beta` is never a witness: its `Q` coordinates *are* `delta`, so
`[Q_i,Q_j](beta) = delta_j*delta_i - delta_i*delta_j = 0` identically.

## 4. Full diagonal locus and the larger equal-`Q`-spectrum locus

The bottom scalar locus satisfies the 843-row operator monomial lattice, so it
lies inside the upper torus `G_m^49`.  Its diagonal copy

```text
E = {a_e = b_e for all 132 coordinates}
```

is therefore a nonempty five-dimensional closed subvariety of the
54-dimensional transverse scalar base, of codimension 49, contained in every
branch's determinant-zero locus.

On `E` we have `delta_Q=0`, so by section 2 all six commutator rows vanish
identically at **every** point of `E`. More strongly, the same conclusion holds
on the generally larger locus

```text
Z_Q = {a_Qi=b_Qi for i=1,2,3,4} = {delta_Q=0}.
```

The whole of `Z_Q` is witness-free, whatever the rank of `M` is. The locus `E`
is the five-dimensional full diagonal sublocus on which rank and kernel were
sampled exactly; it is not asserted to be all of `Z_Q`.

The rank and kernel are then certified pointwise.  At three exact rational
points of `E` -- `t=(1,1,1,1), q5=1/32`; `t=(3,5,7,11), q5=13/4`;
`t=(2/3,5/7,9/4,1/6), q5=7/5` -- all four branch matrices have

```text
rank = 127,   nullity = 5,
```

a drop of four from the generic derived rank 131.  The kernel is identified
exactly:

```text
ker M = span( d b_e/d t1, d b_e/d t2, d b_e/d t3, d b_e/d t4, d b_e/d q5 ),
```

the tangent space of the five-dimensional bottom scalar locus, realised as pure
scalar deformations.  Each of the five vectors annihilates **every** row of
**every** branch matrix, they are linearly independent, and the nullity is
exactly five, so at each of those points they span the kernel and nothing else
does.

Rank constancy along the whole of `E` is *not* claimed.  On `E` the operator and
fixed-GC rows become the integer incidence rows column-scaled by `1/a_e`, but the
MSR rows become integer rows with no such scaling, so the two blocks are not
scaled compatibly and a constancy argument does not go through.  The rank 127 is
an exact pointwise certificate at three well-separated points.

## 5. Two scalar characters and the Q5-only subfamily

Let

```text
S = {the upper character a is itself a bottom scalar locus point}.
```

`S` is nonempty of dimension `5+5=10`, codimension 44, and it is the exact locus
where `beta` enters the kernel, by section 3. The locus `E` is precisely the full
diagonal of `S`, but it is **not** the whole `delta_Q=0` part of `S`, because
external `Q5` is absent from `delta_Q`.

Indeed, let `T` be the six-dimensional subfamily on which the upper and lower
characters share `t1,t2,t3,t4` while their two external `Q5` coordinates vary
independently. Since

```text
Q_n = 1/lambda(n,0),  n=1,2,3,4,
```

equality of `Q_1,...,Q_4` recursively forces equality of the four couplings.
Consequently `T` is exactly the `delta_Q=0` part of `S`; `E` is the
`q5_upper=q5_lower` diagonal inside `T`. The whole of `T` is witness-free by
section 2.

At four exact rational points of the `delta_Q!=0` part of `S`, over the reference bottom character
`t=(3,5,7,11), q5=13/4`, all four branch matrices have

```text
rank = 130,   nullity = 2,   delta != 0,
```

and the kernel is identified exactly:

```text
ker M = span(beta, e_Q5).
```

Both `beta` and `e_Q5` annihilate every row, they are independent, and the
nullity is two.  Both directions commute -- `beta` by the identity of section 3,
`e_Q5` because it has no `Q1,...,Q4` component.  So those four points carry no
witness even though `delta` is nonzero there.

At the exact Q5-only point with shared couplings `t=(3,5,7,11)`, lower
`q5=13/4`, and upper `q5=17/5`, only the `Q5` coordinate differs. All four
branch matrices instead have

```text
rank = 127,   nullity = 5,   delta_Q = 0,
```

and their kernel is exactly the five-dimensional tangent space of the upper
bottom-character locus at that point. This gives a concrete regression against
the false implication `S \ E => delta_Q!=0` and against treating
`span(beta,e_Q5)` as a locus-wide kernel formula.

What is universal on `S` is: `beta` lies in the kernel everywhere; `T` is
witness-free because `delta_Q=0`; and the beta direction never produces a
witness. On the remaining `delta_Q!=0` part, rank 130 and
`ker M=span(beta,e_Q5)` are certified only at the four listed points. Rank
constancy and witness-freedom on that whole remaining part are not proved.

At each of the four nonzero-`delta_Q` points tested, the rank drop is entirely
accounted for by the displayed harmless kernel. A witness search must still
cover the rest of the nonzero-`delta_Q` base, including the unresolved part of
`S` and points outside `S`.

```text
rank = 131 / 131 / 132 / 132
```

in derived-strict / derived-completed / literal-strict / literal-completed.
The four certified splitting principal opens are therefore no longer confined to

These two points also exhibit the coboundary dichotomy of section 3 in its

10 Eq. (139) rows exactly. It fails respectively 224 and 255 of the 320

precisely what the universal identity predicts, and it shows that `beta` is a
kernel direction exactly on the two-scalar locus and nowhere else.

## 8. What is now excluded and what remains

Excluded, stratum-wide:

- the whole equal-`Q`-spectrum locus `Z_Q={delta_Q=0}`;
- in particular the full diagonal locus `E`, dimension 5, and the
  six-dimensional same-couplings independent-Q5 subfamily `T`;
- the coboundary direction `beta` itself, at every point of the whole base.

Excluded, pointwise and exactly:

- four points of the two-scalar locus `S` with `delta_Q != 0`, where the kernel
  is exactly `span(beta,e_Q5)`;
- one point of `T \ E`, where the rank is 127 and the kernel is the upper
  bottom-locus tangent space;
- three points of `E`, where the rank is 127 and the kernel is the bottom
  tangent space;
- every point of the deterministic scan.

Still open:

- whether the `Q`-projection of `ker M` always lies in
  `K_delta=ker C(delta_Q)`, equivalently whether `rank[M;C] = rank M` on the
  whole transverse base; on `delta_Q!=0` this reduces to containment in
  `span(delta_Q)`, while on `delta_Q=0` it is automatic because `K_0=Q^4`;
- whether the rank is constant along `E` and along `S`;
- the four determinant hypersurfaces themselves, which are still not expanded
  over the 54 base parameters;
- the pair-irreducible, triple-irreducible and aligned branches;
- everything outside the transverse globally reducible chart.

The next exact gate is therefore no longer "solve the determinant-zero loci".
It is the sharper question above.

One warning is recorded with it. A natural but wrong reading of sections 3--5
would be "defend the invariant `ker M subset span(beta,e_Q5)`". Section 8.1
below exhibits an exact degenerate point where that fails and there is still no
witness.

### 8.1 `span(beta,e_Q5)` is not the right invariant

Take the scan point in direction `K[13]` at `s=3`.  Its upper character is not a
bottom character, so by section 3 `beta` is not in the kernel at all -- it
violates 120 rows.  The rank is 130, the kernel is two-dimensional, and it is
*not* contained in `span(beta,e_Q5)`.

Yet all six commutator forms vanish on the whole kernel. The reason is section
2: direction `K[13]` has `Q`-column exponents `(0,1,0,0)`, so this particular
point has nonzero `delta_Q=(0,delta_2,0,0)`, and the kernel's `Q` coordinates are
`(0,*,0,0)`. They land on `span(delta_Q)=K_delta`.

So the invariant that actually decides the question is whether the
`Q`-projection of `ker M` lies in `K_delta=ker C(delta_Q)`, not whether the full
kernel lies in the coboundary span. A successor search that defends the wrong
one will chase a locus that is large and harmless.
