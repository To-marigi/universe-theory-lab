## 2. Why a sampled matching is not the proof

Over completely independent diagonal symbols `a_e,b_e`, the selected matrix
has a 559-edge syntactic support graph and more than one perfect matching.
Five of those edges are fixed-vector-GC coefficients whose bottom-prefix
binomials vanish identically on the finite-CSG bottom family.  Direct symbolic
substitution proves all five zeros.  Removing exactly those edges leaves a
554-edge **safe symbolic supergraph** over the actual scalar base.

The word “supergraph” matters: an off-matching nonzero rational function may
still vanish at a special base point.  This causes no problem.  The 554-edge
graph has a unique perfect matching, certified by acyclicity of its alternating
digraph.  Therefore every determinant permutation except the matched one
contains an identically zero entry.  Specialisation can delete off-matching
edges but cannot create a second determinant term.

The five removed entries occur at GC offsets `0,0,6,9,21`.  Their independent-
diagonal formulas and exact row/column locations are retained in the artifact.

