# v0.4.2 721 Groebner basis: bounded preflight, measured infeasible at trivial scale

Status: **preflight only — not a production gate, not registered as a
solver run against the 721 profile**

## Why this exists

The [full exhaustive relation-reduction sweep](v0.4.2_721_gc_tier_relation_reduced.md)
closed nothing over all 1,967 originally scalar-nonzero residuals. The
remaining option identified there was a full Groebner basis computation
over the combined ideal (1,967 residuals + 8 defining relations, ~104
matrix-entry/inverse variables). Before attempting or refusing that, this
preflight measures actual `sympy.groebner()` cost on tiny subsystems, per
this project's own discipline of measuring before declaring infeasibility
(and per its own history: `cpobc_q_presentation_v033.py` carries a
permanent mutation guard against an "unbounded dense 96-variable Groebner
launched" ever running unannounced, and `cpobc_v031.py`/`cpobc_v03.py`/
`cpobc_d2_v032.py` all carry explicit "no dense full-system Groebner
basis" policy notes predating this track).

## Method

Each subprocess call is killed at a hard 90s wall-clock timeout, so a
blow-up cannot hang the session. Subsystems use the *smallest* reduced
CPOBC residuals by term count (favoring the cheapest possible case, not a
representative one) plus only the defining relations for whichever of the
8 inverse tokens actually appear among them — i.e. this measures a
best-case lower bound on cost, not the real 1,967-residual system.

## Result

| residuals in subsystem | variables | wall time | basis size |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 15 | 0.020s | 7 |
| 2 | 15 | 7.750s | 32 |
| 3 | — | **timeout (>90s)** | — |

Going from 1 to 2 of the smallest possible residuals already costs a
~390x jump (0.02s -> 7.75s). Going to 3 exceeds 90s without finishing.
This is the textbook doubly-exponential blow-up Groebner/Buchberger
computations are known for, measured directly on this project's own
smallest, most favorable subsystem — not assumed.

The real target system (1,967 residuals, ~104 variables, residuals up to
32,099 terms and degree 18 after reduction) is vastly larger than the
3-residual, 15-variable case that already would not finish in 90s. A full
unbounded run at that scale is not casually feasible with this project's
plain-Python/sympy tooling, and this is now a measured conclusion, not an
assumed one.

## What this settles and what it does not

This does not prove no Groebner-based approach could ever work — e.g. a
dedicated CAS (Singular, Macaulay2, Sage — none present in this project's
toolchain) with degree-reverse-lex orderings tuned per subsystem, or an
elimination strategy that never forms the full combined ideal at once,
might behave very differently. It shows that the naive full-system attempt
this project's own tooling could run is measured-infeasible at a scale
1,600+ times smaller than the real target, which is sufficient grounds to
not attempt the unbounded run without new tooling or a materially
different strategy.

## Safeguards and scope

This script is deliberately **not** a production gate: no JSON schema, no
semantic digest, no `CURRENT_RESEARCH_STATE.json` solver-run registration
beyond this note, because it does not touch the real 721 profile's
residual inventory — only a synthetic best-case subsystem built to measure
cost safely. Every subprocess call is time-boxed; nothing ran unbounded.
