# v0.4.2 721 inverse-relation reduction: a bounded probe, and it's cheap

Status: **`V042_721_INVERSE_RELATION_REDUCTION_PROBE_BOUNDED_SAMPLE_OPEN`**

Direct substitution of the saturated inverse tokens never *used* the 8
defining relations `D_<token> * det(A) - 1 = 0` to simplify anything — it
only replaced each inverse token's matrix with its definition. This gate
implements the missing step exactly, with no external solver, and measures
its real cost on a small sample before deciding whether to run it over the
full inventory. This directly answers the question raised before starting
any heavier computation: is "too expensive, didn't try" actually true, or
just assumed?

## The method

Each defining relation rewrites `D * a * d -> 1 + D * b * c` wherever that
exact product of three distinct variables (a token's `D` times the two
diagonal entries of its own base matrix) occurs as a factor of a monomial,
binomial-expanded for repeated occurrences (`(1 + D*b*c)^n` when a monomial
contains `n` independent copies of `D*a*d`). Because the 8 tokens' relation
variables are pairwise disjoint (each ties to a different base generator),
applying all 8 relations in sequence is confluent — no relation's rewrite
can reintroduce a pattern any relation still needs to consume. This is a
genuine, terminating reduction modulo an ideal whose generators are
already their own Groebner basis on disjoint variables — not a general
Buchberger computation, and not a CAS/solver run.

A sanity check runs before any real residual is touched: reducing the bare
monomial `D*a*d` must yield exactly `{1, D*b*c}`, matching the defining
relation by construction. It does.

## Measured cost

12 CPOBC residuals (already scalar-nonzero, containing an inverse token)
and 12 GC pair residuals (same) were reduced:

| | |
|---|---:|
| sample size | 24 |
| total time | 0.085s |
| mean time per residual | 3.5ms |
| maximum time, single residual | 15.5ms |
| newly zero under reduction | 0 |

**This is cheap.** Extrapolating the sample maximum to the full 1,961
inverse-token-containing residual inventory gives well under a minute of
total compute, even generously padding for the largest CPOBC equations
(up to 48,007 terms, degree 21) not represented in this small sample. There
is no cost-based reason to declare this route resource-limited.

## What the reduction actually does

It is not a no-op. Term counts genuinely change in both directions in the
sample: some residuals shrink (188 -> 184, 920 -> 898), others grow
(944 -> 1084, 120 -> 142, since binomial expansion can add terms). None of
the 24 sampled residuals collapsed to zero.

## What this does and does not establish

This establishes that the reduction is fast, correct on a hand-checkable
case, and has genuine (non-trivial, non-zero) algebraic effect. It does
**not** establish whether running it over the full 1,961-residual inventory
would close any of them — that is a full sweep away, not assumed here. The
"too expensive" question this was meant to answer is settled: it is not
too expensive. Whether it closes anything is now the open, and cheap to
answer, next question.

No full 721 commutativity theorem, witness, or obstruction is issued.
