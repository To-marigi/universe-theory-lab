## 1. Scientific conclusion

The bounded primary-source search found no paper that states the same finite
weak-semantics result as this project: a nonsingular rational noncommutative
`d=2`, `n<=4` CPOBC witness under fixed-vector GC and reachable-state MSR, or
the complete four-profile semantic comparison sought in v0.4. It also found no
paper stating the exact SR3b-A reconstruction-slice theorem with the project's
source-to-presentation and chart certificates.

This is not an absolute absence claim. The strongest new overlap is Yipeng
Xu, arXiv:2607.26672v1 (2026-07-29). Its Theorem 29.12 contains substantial
CPOBC rigidity results, including finite-dimensional self-adjoint nonsingular
commutativity and several exact two-dimensional triangular/extension no-go
statements. Relative to this project it is:

- `STRICTLY_MORE_GENERAL` on the self-adjoint finite-stage slice;
- `ADJACENT_THEOREM` for the general non-self-adjoint and weak-state semantics;
- not a `SAME_CLAIM` for SR2 or SR3b-A.

Accordingly, the project must not claim the first `d=2` CPOBC rigidity result,
the first CPOBC commutativity theorem, or priority over all self-adjoint or
triangular rigidity. The defensible core is the exact semantic separation:
strong/strong finite rigidity versus a source-native weak/weak rational
noncommutative witness, with the one-sided profiles still open except for the
certified reconstruction slice.

### 2.4 Reachability and on-state controls

- Dowker--Johnston--Sorkin, arXiv:1002.0589v3, Theorem 1 on PDF p. 7 is onto
  only under a nonzero-amplitude-history condition for every final
  configuration. PDF p. 8 includes one-dimensional/proper history-Hilbert
  examples. Reachable vectors therefore do not automatically span an
  independently chosen transition space.
- Czajkowski--Grilo, arXiv:2101.08313v2, Theorem 4 on PDF p. 9 characterizes
  on-state full permutability in its measurement setting. Theorem 5 and the
  PDF pp. 10--11 example show that pairwise on-state commutation need not
  imply higher permutability. This is an adjacent warning, not a CPOBC
  counterexample.

## 3. Closest 2026 CPOBC overlap

### Xu, arXiv:2607.26672v1

Local PDF:
`references/papers/2607.26672v1_xu_relational-quantum-causal-processes.pdf`

PDF pp. 99--107, especially Section 29.11 and Theorem 29.12, were rendered and
visually inspected. The theorem package publicly states, within its declared
hypotheses, that:

1. finite-dimensional self-adjoint nonsingular CPOBC systems commute;
2. no leading noncommutative tangent occurs through four events at its scalar
   base point;
3. upper-triangular extensions between distinct positive scalar CSG
   characters split;
4. equal-character self-extensions are commuting coupling tangents;
5. an irreducible nonsingular `2 by 2` onset has scalar `R_2`, while a
   non-scalar `R_2` is excluded;
6. one trace-balanced subchart is excluded and another chart is left open.

The same section expressly leaves non-self-adjoint nonsingular transitions,
state-only covariance, singular/zero couplings, higher-dimensional nilpotent
thickenings, a remaining first-simple-spectrum chart, and delayed/Jordan
onset open. These boundaries preserve a distinct v0.4 weak-semantics target.

The paper cites a companion manuscript on self-adjoint/all-order triangular
rigidity, but the audit found no independent public arXiv ID, DOI, or
authoritative manuscript URL for it. Do not treat that companion as inspected;
cite the theorem as it appears in arXiv:2607.26672v1.

Chronology matters. Xu v1 was submitted on 2026-07-29. The public Zenodo
v0.3.9 record was created on 2026-07-31 at 12:59 UTC and carries publication
date 2026-07-31. Therefore no public-priority claim over Xu is available for
their overlapping self-adjoint/triangular territory. Repository history may
establish internal chronology, but it is not a substitute for public priority
and should not be used that way in the manuscript.

## 4. Mathematical commutativity toolkit

### 4.1 Two-by-two triangularization and centralizers

Florentino, arXiv:0809.3032v1, was already archived. PDF pp. 4--8 were
visually checked.

- Theorem 1.11 gives a simultaneous-triangularization criterion involving
  individual triangularizability, `det(AB-BA)=0`, and triple trace conditions.
- Theorems 1.12--1.13 reduce the required pair/triple tests for reduced
  families.
- Triangularizable does not mean commuting. In `2 by 2`, vanishing commutator
  determinant detects a common invariant line but permits a nonzero
  square-zero commutator.

Reusable project lemmas:

- for every traceless `2 by 2` matrix `C`,
  `C^2 = -det(C) I = (tr(C^2)/2) I`;
- over a field, the centralizer of a non-scalar `A in M_2(F)` is `F[I,A]`;
  hence proving that every `Q_i` commutes with one non-scalar `Q_k` is a
  compact endpoint for full pairwise commutativity;
- branch early into a common-invariant-line (simultaneously triangularizable)
  locus and an irreducible locus before expensive elimination.

### 4.2 Common eigenvectors and Burnside branching

- Jamiołkowski--Pastuszak, arXiv:1306.0083v1, Theorems 1.1, 1.2, and 2.4
  (PDF pp. 3, 4, and 7) give computable common-eigenvector/invariant-subspace
  criteria. For `2 by 2` pairs over an algebraically closed field, the common
  eigenvector test reduces to the familiar `det[A,B]=0` criterion.
- Yahaghi, arXiv:1507.00377v3, and Radjavi--Yahaghi,
  arXiv:1811.04243v1, supply Burnside-type irreducible/full-matrix-algebra
  dichotomies with explicit field hypotheses.

These results organize the search; they do not eliminate a noncommuting upper
triangular CPOBC branch and therefore cannot replace the profile equations.

The original Shemesh article, *Common eigenvectors of two matrices*, LAA 62
(1984), DOI 10.1016/0024-3795(84)90085-5, is metadata-only in the archive.
Publisher and discovered public endpoints did not provide a lawful
unauthenticated PDF during this audit. The archived arXiv generalization is
used for inspected theorem text.

### 4.3 Separating vectors and the minimal promotion condition

Lledó, arXiv:0901.1004v1, Definition 2.1 and Proposition 2.2 on PDF p. 2 and
Example 2.10 on PDF p. 7 were visually checked.

For this project the correct finite-dimensional statement is not “cyclic
implies strong.” Let `R` be the linear space of residual operators whose
statewise equality is known. A vector `Omega` is `R`-separating exactly when

`D in R and D Omega = 0  =>  D = 0`.

Equivalently, evaluation `R -> H`, `D |-> D Omega`, is injective. This is the
minimal promotion hypothesis for that residual family. Cyclicity alone is
insufficient: every nonzero vector is cyclic for the defining representation
of `M_2`, while a nonzero rank-one operator can still annihilate it. A vector
in `C^2` also cannot separate all of `M_2`, since the evaluation map has a
four-dimensional domain and a two-dimensional codomain.

For reachable-state MSR in `d=2`, a simpler exact recovery condition is:
if the same residual `D_c` annihilates two linearly independent reachable
vectors at source `c`, then `D_c=0`. Thus sourcewise reachable-span rank two
recovers strong MSR. This condition is weaker and more targeted than asking a
single vector to separate the entire transition algebra.

### 4.4 Locally linearly dependent and commuting varieties

- Meshulam--Šemrl, Pacific J. Math. 203 (2002), Theorems 2.2, 2.4, and 2.6
  (journal pp. 444--445 and 450), provide low-rank and square-zero elements in
  suitable locally linearly dependent operator spaces. This supports the
  rank-one/square-zero form of statewise slack but is not a CPOBC theorem.
- Guralnick--Sethuraman, LAA 310 (2000), gives geometry and dimension checks
  for commuting varieties and two-dimensional commutative subalgebras of
  `M_2`. It studies tuples already assumed to commute and cannot prove that a
  weak CPOBC locus is contained in the commuting variety.
- Dubi, *Operators and Matrices* 1 (2007), shows that its weak
  triangularization notion agrees with simultaneous triangularization for
  `2 by 2` matrices. Again, this stops short of commutativity.

## 5. Consequences for implementation and proof strategy

1. Add an exact reducible/irreducible branch test before large Gröbner runs.
   Use simultaneous-triangularization minors and generalized Shemesh tests as
   scouts or certified branch predicates, with field assumptions recorded.
2. Form the profile-specific residual space and test injectivity of
   `D |-> D Omega`; do not demand a separating vector for the full transition
   algebra unless that stronger statement is independently intended.
3. Treat sourcewise rank-two reachable span as the clean minimal recovery of
   strong MSR in `d=2`.
4. Use the centralizer of one certified non-scalar `Q_k` as a possible short
   endpoint: show every `Q_i` lies in `F[I,Q_k]`.
5. Keep noncommuting triangular branches alive until CPOBC/GC/MSR eliminate
   them; `det[Q_i,Q_j]=0` alone is not a commutativity certificate.
6. Continue the 955 search in the remaining remote/disconnected
   `x!=0,y!=0` components. Xu's open non-self-adjoint/state-only boundaries
   make this scientifically worthwhile, but do not change the existing
   exact-coverage gates.
